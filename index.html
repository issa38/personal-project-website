Four steps, in this order. Skipping one is where projects bleed time.

The same loop, whether the problem is a valuation, a workflow audit, or a process nobody owns. It is not glamorous, but it is why the work sticks.

How I start
With the work itself. Before tools, before slides, I study what people actually do and where it lags.
Where I lean technical
Modeling and AI agents, used when they earn their place. A modeled spreadsheet beats a pitch built on estimates.
Where I lean human
Lean and Kaizen. Small, honest improvements the team is still using six months later.
What I am chasing
Compounding value: the small bets that get more valuable the longer they stay in the system.

  1. Watch the work first

    Who hands what to whom, what gets redone, and what is secretly held together by one person's inbox.

  2. Put a dollar on the gap

    Friction is easy to ignore until it is priced. Time lost, decisions delayed, risk carried. Pick a number.

    See the activation opportunity math

  3. Build the smallest fix that works

    Sometimes a checklist. Sometimes Python. Sometimes an agent. The tool comes last, not first.

    See the model architecture

  4. Hand it off so it survives me

    If the team cannot run it without me in the room, it is not a system. It is a favor.

Activation Funnel Redesign Lab

Growth analytics A/B test design Revenue sizing

GA4 BigQuery ecommerce analysis that finds the highest-leverage activation gap, prices the revenue opportunity, designs the A/B test, and ends in a one-page impact memo.

The finding
New users are 89% of visitors but activate at 0.64%, versus 9.73% for returning users.
The opportunity
A +50% relative lift implies 769 incremental purchasers and $53,130 in revenue.
The action
Run Variant B first, with a one-page impact memo explaining the math and risks.
